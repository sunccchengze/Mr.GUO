## 摘要

Air-traffic regulations and environmental concerns motivate alternative gas-turbine cycles as Joule-based turbofans approach theoretical efficiency limits. A multi-architecture engine model spans a conventional two-shaft turbofan through a three-stream reheated, intercooled and recuperated core. It incorporates rotating-detonation-combustion-based reheating, controllable turbine cooling, and optimized bleed extraction. A mixed-integer nonlinear model with more than 350 continuous and binary variables optimizes eight core design features and estimated weight using a multi-start, physics-acquainted branch-and-bound algorithm. The resulting architectures achieve higher efficiency than conventional high-bypass-ratio turbofans; reheating raises thrust at comparable core and secondary flows without major weight addition.
