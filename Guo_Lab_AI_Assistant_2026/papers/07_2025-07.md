## 摘要

Turbine design optimization is critical for efficient performance and longevity. A significant challenge is analyzing creep and thermo-mechanical fatigue to exploit the design space robustly. This paper presents a deep-learning approach to model the relationship between geometry and boundary-condition deviations of a turbine blade and its lifing in 3-D. Using simulated data and relevant material properties, the models predict creep impact under geometry deviations and different operating conditions. A process chain generates deviations by stacking and rotating the blade airfoil, changing pressure- and suction-side wall thickness, and varying cooling-air supply and hot-gas boundary conditions. The resulting data train a deep-learning surrogate whose predictive performance is evaluated. The AI-driven approach identifies design parameters that minimize adverse effects of geometry deviation, with the aim of improved turbine performance and extended service life.
